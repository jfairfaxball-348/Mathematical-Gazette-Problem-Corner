## Corpus progress

The working catalogue contains **21 solution-reviewed problems**. Statement years represented are **2019, 2024, 2025, 2026**, across seven issue contexts: July 2019; July and November 2024; March, July and November 2025; and March 2026.

The tranche was selected for variety rather than as one convenient consecutive run. Mechanisms represented include probability/random walks, combinatorics and generating functions, Euclidean and coordinate geometry, triangle centres, modular number theory, recurrences, inequalities/convexity, complex numbers, definite integrals, asymptotics, Gamma/Beta functions, zeta values, polynomial-root methods, and induction/invariants.

Presentation mix:
- abstract: 16
- everyday context: 3
- light context: 1
- mixed: 1

Across all 21 published solutions:

These figures are descriptive only and should not drive Stage 3.

## Patterns worth testing on a larger corpus

1. Statement economy and solution depth are often asymmetric: short statements can support long published solutions.
2. Change of representation recurs as a source of elegance: modular recoding, complexification, infinite products, and polynomial-root encodings all appear.
3. Alternate solutions matter editorially; commentary often singles out a cleaner route or prints a generalisation.
4. Contextual examples are a minority here but can produce especially strong qualitative surprise; 108.L, 109.E and 110.C are notable.
5. Problem Corner is much broader than an accessible-puzzle section; this tranche includes Gamma functions, zeta values, Karamata/majorization and Feuerbach-point geometry.
6. Extensions are culturally common: editors leave variants, report solver generalisations, or invite further development.

## Sampling and access limitations

The dominant weakness is editor/era imbalance: **20 of 21** records concern Chris Starr-era problems posed in 2024–2026; only 103.H represents Nick Lord. This tranche is therefore not evidence for long-run editorial frequency.

Searchability also favours recent freely accessible material. The sample is too small and recent to estimate how often everyday/qualitative framing occurs historically.
