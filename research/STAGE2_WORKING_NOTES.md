## Testing the first-tranche observations against older evidence

The new evidence is small, so all conclusions remain provisional.

1. **Short statement / longer solution asymmetry — supported.** Both 99.C and 101.L have compact statements but published discussions extending across substantial solution text.
2. **Alternate solutions matter editorially — strongly supported.** 99.C prints two elementary bounding routes and several geometric variants; 101.L explicitly notes vectors, complex numbers, and trilinear/areal coordinates.
3. **Editorial extensions and generalisations — strongly supported.** 99.C is extended from a specific angle-bisector comparison to arbitrary concurrent cevians. 101.L is extended from centroid/Lemoine-point ratios to a fact about any two distinct interior points.
4. **Contextual/everyday framing frequency — not supported by these additions.** Both older additions are abstract geometry, so the corpus becomes slightly more abstract rather than less. This may reflect repository discoverability and must not be treated as a historical frequency estimate.
5. **Prerequisite range — supported as broad.** 99.C has an elementary core using the angle-bisector theorem and AM-GM; 101.L uses specialist triangle-centre vocabulary but admits standard vector/coordinate methods.
6. **Recurring mechanisms — partially supported.** Change of representation remains important, but the stronger older signal here is that a specific problem often opens into a clean general theorem through solver response.
7. **Cleaner alternative routes highlighted by the editor — supported.** The printed discussions preserve multiple proofs/variants rather than presenting only one canonical derivation.

## Sampling and access limitations

The principal weakness remains editor/era imbalance: **20 of 23** records are Chris Starr-era, while only **3 of 23** are Nick Lord-era. This is an improvement from 20 of 21, but it is nowhere near balanced enough for the Stage-2 gate.

The requested older-corpus tranche is **not complete**. Only 2 of the requested at least 24 new pre-2024 records were added in this session. They come from two distinct older issue contexts.

Searchability continues to favour recent freely accessible material and isolated institutional deposits. Several older issue contexts can be identified confidently from Cambridge, Taylor & Francis, JSTOR and Mathematical Association/WPR metadata, but solution review must wait until legitimate full published pages are actually accessible. Do not promote metadata-only items into the catalogue.

## Precise next research step

Continue the same older-corpus tranche. Add **at least 22 more solution-reviewed pre-2024 problems from at least four additional issue contexts**, so that the tranche reaches its original minimum of 24 records across six or more contexts.

Concentrate on non-consecutive sampling across **2010–2023**, especially Nick Lord-era issues, and deliberately seek:
- contextual/everyday as well as abstract problems;
- elementary and advanced prerequisites;
- short and substantial solutions;
- single-solution and multiple-solution/editor-commentary cases;
- mathematical areas not already overrepresented by geometry and analysis.

Prefer full Cambridge Core/Taylor & Francis archive pages or PDFs. Where publisher full text is gated, use only legitimate institutional/repository copies that can be verified as the published Gazette pages, and record that provenance explicitly.

Also repair exact statements for 110.B and 110.D if legitimate publisher PDF access becomes available.

Even after the remaining 22-record tranche, the corpus would be only about 45 records, so Stage 2 should still remain in progress pending the roughly-60 minimum, balance checks, final synthesis, genre comparison, and evidence-based Stage-3 design specification.

No candidate generation, scoring, preferred-field selection, or novelty audit should begin.
