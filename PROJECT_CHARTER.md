# Project Charter

## Publication target

The sole publication target is **The Mathematical Gazette — Problem Corner**.

The project exists to develop an original, accessible, apparently qualitative mathematical problem that is genuinely attractive for that section.

## Frozen design principle

> **simple situation → natural question → unexpected answer → elegant mathematics**

The surface should ideally be understandable to a mathematically interested reader without specialist vocabulary. Ordinary contexts may involve people, choices, food, queues, seats, journeys, cards, objects, voting, sharing, games, routes, schedules, rankings, swaps, information, guessing, probabilities, arrangements, or other familiar situations.

The mathematics underneath may be rigorous and non-trivial. The project does **not** choose its mathematical field in advance.

## Explicit non-objectives

The objective is **not**:

- a difficult theorem for its own sake;
- a frontier research paper;
- an unsolved conjecture with no known solution;
- a specialist problem requiring substantial definitions before it can even be stated;
- a generic internet brainteaser;
- a known puzzle with cosmetic changes.

Nor is the project primarily trying to discover a deep specialist theorem, solve an open conjecture, produce a long research paper, or force the problem into number theory.

## Desired qualities of the eventual problem

The ultimate candidate should ideally have:

- immediate comprehensibility;
- puzzle appeal;
- genuine mathematical substance;
- a surprising result or mechanism;
- an elegant complete solution;
- compact wording;
- opportunity for reader experimentation;
- strong originality evidence;
- strong fit to the actual Gazette Problem Corner corpus.

These are project success criteria, not optional stylistic preferences.

## Method

The project deliberately separates editorial research, invention, novelty checking, and final formalisation.

Stage 2 must learn the section from a substantial corpus before serious invention begins. Stage 4 must investigate both story-level and mathematically equivalent prior art. Stage 5 formalisation means mathematical precision and rigorous validation; use of a proof assistant is optional and justified only if it adds real value.

The final act of sending a submission remains a human action unless explicitly requested later.
