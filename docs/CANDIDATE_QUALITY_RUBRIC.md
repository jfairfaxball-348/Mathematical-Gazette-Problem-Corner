# Candidate Quality Rubric

**Status:** frozen in Stage 1; do not score invented candidates until Stage 3.

Later candidates must be assessed on all ten dimensions below. A simple 1–5 scale may be used in Stage 3, where 1 is clearly poor for the target and 5 is exceptionally strong. Stage 2 may refine evidence-based interpretation of what the scores mean, but may not silently remove or replace a dimension.

## Dimensions

1. **Immediate comprehensibility** — Can an interested reader grasp the situation and question quickly?
2. **Puzzle appeal** — Does the question create genuine curiosity and invite engagement?
3. **Surprise** — Is the answer, phenomenon, or route meaningfully unexpected rather than merely obscure?
4. **Mathematical depth** — Is there enough real mathematical substance beneath the surface?
5. **Solution elegance** — Is there a clean, satisfying argument rather than an ugly rescue operation?
6. **Statement economy** — Is the wording compact, natural, and free of unnecessary machinery?
7. **Experimentability** — Can readers test examples, simulate small cases, draw pictures, or otherwise explore productively?
8. **Accessibility** — Are prerequisites and notation appropriate for the Gazette readership and Problem Corner setting?
9. **Originality** — Is there strong evidence that the mathematical problem is not simply known prior art in a new costume?
10. **Gazette fit** — Does the problem resemble, in the right ways, the qualities evidenced by the actual Problem Corner corpus?

## Warning signs

Treat these as explicit review prompts:

- tedious casework;
- arbitrary constants selected only to make a result work;
- dependence on brute-force computation;
- specialist notation;
- disguised textbook exercise;
- trivial trick answer;
- artificial story pasted onto an equation;
- ugly solution to an attractive-looking problem;
- excessive setup;
- known problem with renamed objects.

Other warning signs may be added later, but these may not be removed without an explicit project-level decision.

## Use in Stage 3

A high total score is not sufficient by itself. A candidate with a fatal originality problem, unsound mathematics, no credible solution, or a severe Gazette-fit failure must not survive merely because other dimensions score well.

Likewise, the rubric should not be used as a substitute for judgement. Preserve short written reasons, especially for surprise, elegance, originality risk, and Gazette fit.
